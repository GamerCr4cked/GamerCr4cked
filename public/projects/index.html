Here are some of my personal projects. They cover web development, Minecraft server ideas, and experimenting with software. Some are public, while others are still being developed or are intended for a small community.

01 / WEB DEVELOPMENT01

Tidewritten

A light-novel reading website that I'm developing as a place to organize and read story editions.

The site is designed around a series-and-volume library rather than a list of unrelated chapters. Its reader focuses on a comfortable reading layout, theme controls, reading progress, and language switching for editions that have translations available.

I'm also working on consistent chapter formatting, navigation between volumes, and a visual identity built around dark colors and aquatic cyan accents.

02 / MINECRAFT NETWORK02

GC-Network

A Minecraft server project planned around Java and Bedrock players.

The idea includes cross-platform multiplayer, an economy, land-claim features, and a community-focused server setup. Working on it has involved learning about server hosting, configuration, plugins, and how different gameplay systems fit together.

It is also a practical way for me to explore server administration and the technical side of running a multiplayer Minecraft environment.

03 / PRIVATE SMP03

PowerCoreSMP

A private survival multiplayer concept for friends and invited creators.

PowerCoreSMP is built around a custom core/ability concept. The design has explored elemental themes such as Fire, Ice, Nature, Ocean, Storm, and Lunar, with the goal of giving players different ways to interact with the world.

The project also has a player guide, and its development involves balancing gameplay ideas, explaining systems clearly, and planning the experience for a small community.

04 / LOCAL AI EXPERIMENT04

GC-AI

A small local AI experiment focused on learning how language models can be run on personal hardware.

The experiment uses Python, llama.cpp, and a compact Qwen instruction model through a local interface. It has helped me explore model setup, local inference, and the practical limits of running AI on a low-spec computer.
